Each change does only what it says here, and nothing else.

Puts whole lines in order, by UK English dictionary order. No line’s characters change. Spaces at the start of a line are ignored when comparing. Blank lines go to the end.
Order
Removes a list marker at the start of a line, and the space after it: • - * – 1. 1) (1) a) (a) (i). Indentation stays. Numbers and symbols anywhere else are untouched.
Changes capitals only. Code cannot know that “london” is a name, so sentence case lists the words it could not decide and leaves them to you.
Change to
A capital at the start of each line and sentence, and for “I”, the days and the months (not March or May, which are also ordinary words). In text that already has lower case, acronyms like NHS are kept.
A capital on each word except a, an, and, as, at, but, by, for, from, in, into, nor, of, on, or, the, to, with.
Repeated spaces become one; spaces at line ends and extra blank lines go. Removes soft hyphens, zero-width and control characters and �. Non-breaking spaces become spaces, and ligatures such as ﬁ become letters. Each kind is counted. Tabs, letters and punctuation never change.
Changes line breaks, and the spaces next to them, by one exact rule. It never decides where a paragraph of prose should start: that is a judgement about meaning.
Rule
Removes line breaks inside paragraphs. Blank lines, and lines that start with a list marker, keep their breaks. A word split by a hyphen at a line end is joined and listed for you to check.
A break before each • and before numbering that runs 1. 2. 3., a) b) c) or (i) (ii) (iii) within one line. A lone number, a dash or an asterisk is not split.
Such as “end.Next”: a lower-case letter, then . ! or ?, then a capitalised word. Web addresses are left alone.
A break after . ! ? or … when a space and then a capital letter follow. Not after a short form such as Mr, Dr, Prof, St, No, Fig, approx, e.g. or i.e., or after a single initial.

      Models used: none

      Every change is made by ordinary code inside this page. No model, local or remote, reads or rewrites your text, and the page is not allowed to send it anywhere, not even to the small local server that opened this window.

      What this does not do, and what is kept

      It does not fix grammar or spelling, reword, summarise or decide where paragraphs belong. Those are judgements about meaning, not exact changes. Comparing two lists never guesses that two different spellings are the same person or thing.

      Your text and results stay in this window’s memory only. Nothing is stored; closing or reloading the window loses them. Line endings from Windows files become plain line breaks. Save or copy what you want to keep.
