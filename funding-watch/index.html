Tools · Funding watch

What changed on your funders' pages this week

Every Monday morning, the funding watch checks the public pages of a charity's chosen funders and emails what changed: a round that opened, an upcoming deadline, a new grant limit. Every date and amount has to appear in the funder's own page text.

Live since October 2026 for its first charity, a small Hackney food pantry where I'm a trustee. They use it free, and always will.

Why this exists

Small charities mostly find funding by luck: a search, a newsletter, word of mouth. Funders publish what they fund and when, on their own websites, in their own phrasing, and change them without warning. Nobody at a tiny charity has time to check 30 funders' websites every Monday. Watching pages and flagging what changed is a repeatable job, and that's what the watch does.

What the email shows

Opened
A round that wasn't open last week and is now.
Closing
A deadline coming up in the next four weeks.
Changed
A date, amount or limit that shifted since last week.
Couldn't read
Any page that failed to load or blocked the watch, with the reason in plain words — nothing gets quietly skipped.

Each item shows the fact, the exact sentence it came from and a link back to the page. A quiet week still sends a one-line email, and if a run breaks, a short "didn't run" email says so.

See a sample email — the real template, populated with invented funders and an invented charity.

How it checks what it shows

  • Every date, amount and area has to appear in the page text. A sentence is quoted only if it matches the page word for word; otherwise the email says "Check the page".
  • Unknown stays unknown. If a page doesn't state a fact, the email says "Not stated on the page" instead of guessing.
  • No verdicts. It never tells a charity it qualifies or drafts an application. At most it flags a round worth checking alongside the funder's wording. People make the call.
  • Unchanged pages aren't re-read. That avoids paying for redundant model runs and stops a model paraphrasing the same page slightly differently from looking like a real change.

What's been tested

  • Finding new funders: Before building the watch, pulling public data on past grants in Hackney turned up 26 funders. The charity marked 11 of them new.
  • Reading pages vs plain code: On real funder pages, the reading model extracted 45 of 55 stated facts; a plain parser found 28.
  • Running cost: A full first read across every page cost 2 to 3p. After that, it only reads pages that changed.

Cloudflare swapped the hosted reading model on 18 November 2026. Re-scored on the earlier pages, the new version matched or beat the old one on dates and amounts, though it sometimes picks up a geographic area mentioned elsewhere on the page — so check the area.

Help open now

A second weekly email for the same charity uses the same reader to track local support schemes with open windows (cost-of-living help, holiday places) and nearby food support. The charity marked 35 of 39 items new to them, and in a blind check its food listings came back 19 out of 19 right.

Models used

Reading facts in running text
Cloudflare's hosted reading model, @cf/cloudflare/clairic, via Workers AI. It reads one fact at a time and returns the sentence it came from. Since 18 November 2026 that name points to the 27.8B version (clairic-27b), and every stored fact records which version read it. Open weights under Apache 2.0: Cloudflare/clairic at revision 2c161be, published 18 November 2026.
Sorting into topics
Keywords first. A second Cloudflare model, @cf/cloudflare/clef, checks only whether core costs are funded, where testing showed it caught wording keywords missed. Open weights under Apache 2.0: Cloudflare/clef at revision 171287b, published 10 November 2026.
Clear, labelled details
Plain code parsing dates, amounts and explicit labels like "Deadline: 3 March". No model.

Cloudflare can swap the model behind a hosted name without warning — as happened on 18 November 2026 — which is why the watch logs the reported version alongside every fact. The links above point to the published weights; the hosted service is Cloudflare's, so the watch records whatever version string it reports.

What stays where

The watch only reads public web pages. Nothing from the charity goes to the cloud: no client records, no draft bids, no accounts. Anything touching a charity's own documents stays on a local computer.

What's unresolved

Only one charity uses it so far, and no grant has come out of it yet. That's the real test still ahead.
