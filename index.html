Kettle Cups

The small jobs you’d give an AI chatbot, done on your own computer.

Kettle is a collection of small tools. Some run small AI models locally, with nothing sent to the cloud. Some need no AI at all, only ordinary code.

Each tool is a Cup: it does one job, gives you a result you can check, and lets you save it. Each is published with a check of what it replaces and where it fails.

Tools

Some run small, specialist AI models on your computer. Some need only ordinary code. Every tool page names the models it uses, or says that it uses none.

  • Tables turns a photo of a printed table into a CSV, using four small models of about 34 MB in all, and reads PDF bank statements with no model.
  • Meeting Notes transcribes a recorded meeting with a speech model on your computer, for you to listen back, correct and save.
  • Text sorts and tidies text with ordinary code. No model at all.
  • Share Copy takes the personal details out of a letter or email before you paste it into a chat model.
  • Regulars lists what you pay for again and again from a year of statements, with ordinary code.

Each one gives you a draft to check. Setup downloads any models once; after that, normal use sends nothing to the cloud. See what every tool page tells you.

Local or remote

For a repeatable task, what is the smallest thing that does it well enough: ordinary code, a small specialist model, a local language model or a remote model? Any of them can be the right answer, and so can what your computer already does.

Useful local models are often not language models. On one tested computer, an Apple M1 Pro, the Tables reader took about a second for each of seven small tables and used about 550 MB of memory at its peak.

Research

Open questions, with dated results. You do not need them to use a tool.

The first study tested my own idea. I gave a frontier agent a guide to Tables, expecting it to use less model work than reading the image itself. On seven small tables it used more. I published the result, the limits of the test and the token budget for one table that came out of it.
