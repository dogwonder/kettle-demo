Tools · iPhone

Read what's in front of you, on your phone

One app for iPhone and iPad holding three small tools. Each reads something you photograph, record or share, checks the result on the phone, and lets you copy, save or hear it read aloud. Nothing leaves the phone, and your originals stay untouched.

In development. Not out for download yet — I run it on my own iPhone and iPad.

The tools

Label
Photograph a UK or EU food label. Allergens appear first, followed by additives, whether it's vegan based on ingredients and additives, and nutrition — with the arithmetic checked and scaled to a portion or part of the pack. Written to sound right when read aloud.
Text
Pick a photo or screenshot and copy its words out as paragraphs, a list or a table. I've used it on my own phone since 19 October 2026.
Scan
Photograph a letter or form page by page into one PDF, each page cropped to the paper. Built, not properly tested yet.

How it's built

  • No Apple Intelligence required. Most iPhones out there can't run Apple's on-device language models, including an iPhone 14. Everything here works without them.
  • No networking code in the app. Photos only come through the camera or photo picker. Copied text stays on your phone unless you've enabled Universal Clipboard ("Copy to my other devices").
  • Refusals instead of guesses. If a photo is too blurry or cropped to read reliably, the tool says "Unproven" and tells you what to retake.
  • Code does the maths; models only classify. Each tool's logic lives in a Swift package with its own unit tests — 252 in total — running on a Mac without needing a phone.
  • Cited nutrition tables. Label checks against the Food Standards Agency's additive register (331 additives) and Regulation (EU) 1169/2011 for reference intakes.

Models used

Reading text in a photo
Apple's built-in Vision framework, part of iOS. Nothing to download.
Language models
None. If Kettle adds Apple's on-device language model later as an optional extra on phones that support it, it will be listed here first.
