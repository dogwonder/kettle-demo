Tools · Media

Resize, crop or trim a file without uploading it.

Choose a video, a picture or a recording. Pick the change yourself, or type what you want and check the settings Media suggests. It shows a written plan, makes nothing until you confirm, and saves a separate file. Your original is never altered.

Why this exists

Shrinking a video, resizing a picture or cutting the start off a recording are small jobs that often mean uploading the file to a website or a chatbot. The work itself is ordinary code that has existed for years. Media runs that code on my own computer. The one new part is that I can type what I want in plain words instead of learning the settings.

What goes in and what comes out

Goes in
One video or recording at a time, or up to 100 pictures. A short landscape MP4 video, JPEG or PNG pictures, or a WAV or MP3 recording.
Video
A smaller copy at 1080p, 720p, 480p or 360p. The whole frame, the full length and the sound are kept.
Picture
A copy that fits inside a width and height you enter, keeping the whole picture. Or a crop from the centre to an exact size. Either can be made black and white.
Recording
The part between a start and an end time you enter. The sound is not re-encoded, so nothing is lost in quality.
Or type it
Describe what you want, such as “fit inside 800 x 600 and make it black and white”, and ask for a suggestion. Media fills in the settings for you to check. A suggestion never makes a plan, starts work or replaces a number you typed.
The plan
Says exactly what will be made, including the size in pixels or the times kept. Changing the file, the request or a setting discards it.
Comes out
A separate file. Media checks it against the plan before offering it. Those checks are mechanical, so look at it or listen to it yourself.

Plain requests are read by a few ordinary rules. Other wording goes to a language model on your computer, which picks from the settings Media has. Code reads every number and time in your request and does the sums. If the model says part of your request is something Media cannot do, nothing is filled in, the part is shown as not done, and you choose the settings yourself.

Without the model, everything else still works. You choose the settings by hand.

Models used

Qwen3 4B Instruct 2507, Q4_K_M
A language model by Alibaba’s Qwen team, in a compressed file published by Unsloth. Its one job is to read a typed request the plain rules could not, and choose settings from a fixed form. It sees only the words you type, never your picture, video or recording, and it cannot write a number of its own. 2,497,281,120 bytes (about 2.5 GB), Apache-2.0 licence, revision a06e946bb6b655725eafa393f4a9745d460374c9, SHA-256 3605803b982cb64aead44f6c1b2ae36e3acdb41d8e46c8a94c6533bc4c67e597. The file’s size and hash are checked before it loads. It runs in llama.cpp, version 10050.
No model
Every change to a file is ordinary code: FFmpeg 8.1.1 for video and MP3, Pillow 12.3.0 for pictures, and plain Python for WAV. Reading a plain request, checking the plan and checking the result are ordinary code too.

The model is optional and loads only when a request needs it. Setup downloads it once; after that, normal use downloads nothing and sends nothing.

What has been checked

Automated tests run on invented pictures, sounds and one invented video clip.

I played back one video copy at 720p and passed it. The other three sizes have had mechanical checks only. I looked at six resized copies of invented test pictures and listened to six trimmed copies of invented test sounds, and passed them all. Cropping and black and white have had mechanical checks only.

Typed requests have been checked on requests written by AI models, not by people. In one run with the language model, the suggestion was complete for 28 of 30 recording requests, 22 of 24 video requests and 28 of 30 picture requests. In a later run of 36 new requests written by a different AI agent, 29 got the settings asked for, three got a wrong setting and four missed a setting Media could have filled in. The second study has the earlier comparison of rules and models.

I have typed requests of my own and they worked for me. I kept no count. Nobody else has tried it.

Known limits

  • A suggestion can be wrong, and can be wrong without saying so. It has swapped a width and a height. Read the plan before you confirm it.
  • Video is narrow: a landscape MP4 of up to 60 seconds and about 64 MB, in the common H.264 format at 30 frames a second. Anything else is refused with the reason. It cannot trim a video.
  • Pictures are JPEG or PNG only. A crop always keeps the centre.
  • Recordings are WAV or MP3 only, one part at a time, with no fade. A trimmed MP3 starts silent or quieter for a fraction of a second, and each cut can land up to about 0.013 seconds from the time you entered. The plan gives both figures for your file.
  • The language model is a 2.5 GB download. It took about a second for each recording request on an Apple M1 Pro.
  • It has only been run on an Apple silicon Mac.
  • A working copy of your file, and each copy you make, stay in a folder on your computer until you delete them.

Availability

Not available to download yet. Media runs in the development Kettle app on my Mac, and as its own page. There is no public installer or source download at present.
