Tools · Meeting Notes

A meeting transcript you can listen back to and check

Pick a recording and a local speech model drafts the transcript on your Mac. Play the audio beside the text, fix whatever it mangled, and save the transcript as a plain text file.

How it works

  1. Pick a recording. An English two-person meeting, up to 30 minutes and 250 MB, in WAV, MP3, M4A, OGG, FLAC or WebM. Your original file isn't touched.
  2. Transcribe it. Whisper runs on your CPU. You can play the audio while it works, or cancel mid-run.
  3. Listen and fix. The transcript sits in an editable box next to the player, with 5-second skip buttons. Watch for dropped or invented words, names, numbers and flipped negatives ("did" vs "didn't"). It doesn't label speakers; type names in yourself if you want them.
  4. Tick and save. Any edit clears the review checkbox. Once you've checked the transcript against the recording, tick the box and save it as text.

While you're editing, the app saves a working copy of the transcript locally so a crash or restart can recover your work (it never copies the audio file). That working copy isn't encrypted, and gets deleted as soon as you save, discard or decline to restore it.

What it leaves out

Meeting Notes doesn't write summaries, pull out action items or guess who's talking. I tried extracting actions from transcripts and got mostly noise: roughly three in four suggestions weren't actions, even with a small model ranking them. Putting a hand-written action list beside the transcript just pulled attention away from checking the words, so I cut it and stopped at the transcript.

If you want a summary or action list, run the transcript through Share Copy to strip personal details, then paste that copy into whichever chat model you like.

Both the upstream model and faster-whisper use MIT licences. PyAV 18.1.0 decodes audio, downmixes to mono and resamples to 16 kHz. Voice activity detection is off, and no speaker-ID, summary or action model runs.

What's been tested

On an Apple M1 Pro, three public meetings from the AMI Meeting Corpus took 82 seconds for 17 minutes of audio, 118 seconds for 25 minutes and 132 seconds for 30 minutes. Each was run once from the command line rather than through the app, and memory wasn't measured. The target for a 30-minute meeting was around five minutes, so speed was comfortable.

Those AMI recordings have four speakers, which is busier than what this tool aims for. Background noise, accents, names and people talking over each other still trip Whisper up, so you have to listen back before trusting a transcript.

Status and what's next

Not available to download yet. Meeting Notes runs in the development Kettle app on macOS.

Transcript accuracy and how long it actually takes to clean up a real two-person meeting haven't been measured yet — that's the main gap before doing anything else with it.
