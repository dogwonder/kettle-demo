Tools · Meeting Notes

A meeting transcript you can listen back to and check.

Choose a recording and a small speech model on your computer writes a draft transcript. Play the recording beside it, correct what it got wrong, then save the transcript as text.

What happens

  1. Choose a recording. An English meeting between two people, up to 30 minutes and 250 MB, as WAV, MP3, M4A, OGG, FLAC or WebM. Your original file is not changed.
  2. Transcribe it. The speech model runs on your computer’s CPU. The recording stays playable while you wait, and you can stop the job.
  3. Listen and correct. The transcript is an editable text box beside the player, with buttons to go back or forward 5 seconds. Listen for missing or invented words, names, numbers and negatives. Speaker names are not added; you can type them in.
  4. Tick and save. Any edit clears the review checkbox. Once you tick that you have checked the transcript against the recording, save it as a text file.

While you work, the transcript is kept in the app’s storage on your computer, so a crash or restart can offer to bring it back. The recording itself is never copied. That stored copy is not encrypted, and it is removed when you save, discard or decline to restore it.

What it does not do

It does not write summaries or action lists, or name who is speaking. I tried suggesting actions from the transcript and it was mostly noise: about three in four suggestions were not actions, even with a small model ranking them. A hand-written action list beside the transcript distracted from checking it. So Meeting Notes stops at the transcript.

If you want a summary or actions, use Share Copy to remove the details you would rather not send, then paste the copy into a chat model of your choice.

The upstream model and the transcription software declare MIT licences. Audio is decoded by PyAV 18.1.0, downmixed to mono and resampled to 16 kHz. Speech detection is disabled. No speaker-identification, summary or action-extraction model is active.

What has been checked

On one computer, an Apple M1 Pro, three public meetings from the AMI Meeting Corpus took 82 seconds for 17 minutes of audio, 118 seconds for 25 minutes and 132 seconds for 30 minutes. That is one run each from the command line, not through the app, and memory was not measured. My target for a 30-minute meeting was about five minutes.

Those meetings have four speakers, more than this tool is meant for. How accurate the transcripts are, and how long correcting them takes, has not been measured. Noise, accents, names and people talking over each other can all cause missing or invented words, so a transcript needs checking against the recording before you rely on it.

Availability

Not available to download yet. Meeting Notes runs in the development Kettle app on my Mac. There is no public installer or source download at present.
