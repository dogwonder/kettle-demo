Tools · Regulars

See what you pay for again and again

Feed in a year of bank statements you've checked in Tables, and Regulars pulls out every payee you pay repeatedly, sorted by what they cost you over the year. Plain code does the maths, so the same statements always produce the same list. The full workflow is written up in A year of statements.

Try Regulars in your browser

The real page from the app, on prepared samples. It's plain code, so it runs for real in your browser. It won't open your own files, and it needs JavaScript.

Why this exists

Some banking apps group spending by merchant; plenty of high-street banks only show vague categories. When I ran an early script over a year of my own statements, the yearly totals were the piece my bank hid from me: direct debits, subscriptions and small habits like coffee, all ranked in one list.

What goes in and what comes out

Goes in
Checked statement CSVs saved from Tables — roughly twelve files for a year. Load them together or add more as you go.
A line
Any payee you paid 3 or more times across 3 or more calendar months. Outgoing payments only.
Each line shows
Rhythm (such as "monthly" or "about 3 times a week"), typical payment, yearly total, any price changes with dates, and when you last paid if it's been a while.
You can
Expand a line to inspect every payment behind it, rename it, merge two names the bank formatted differently, or split them back apart. The bank's original strings stay visible.
New in the latest statement
Every payee appearing for the first time in your newest statement, largest first. "First time" just means absent from the earlier files you loaded — it flags what's new without lecturing you about it.
Comes out
The summary list as CSV, plus the underlying payments and new-payee list if you want them.

Grouping runs on general cleanup rules, like stripping reference numbers and the town name tacked onto card payments. There's no merchant database, no categories, no "waste" badges and no budgeting advice. It shows you what you paid; what you do about it is your business.

Regulars also re-checks each statement's running balance and highlights any row that doesn't add up so you can fix it back in Tables, and warns if two files overlap in dates so payments aren't double-counted.

Models used

None. Every name, date and amount comes straight from your statements, and every total is plain arithmetic. Nothing downloads and nothing leaves your Mac.

What's been tested

Automated tests run on a year of invented statements, including with the network off.

On twelve of my own statements from one bank, every running balance added up and the list needed zero manual merges. Each of the last three statements turned up 22 to 25 first-time payees, most under £20.

Known limits

  • It only understands one bank's column layout so far; adding another bank takes a small parser update.
  • It's only as clean as the CSVs you checked in Tables.
  • Nothing is saved between sessions — closing the window clears your statements, renames and merges, so export the CSV if you want to keep it.
  • You can't merge a payee with fewer than 3 payments in 3 months into a regular line unless it's new in the latest statement.

Status and what's next

Not available to download yet. Regulars runs alongside Tables in the development Kettle app on macOS.
